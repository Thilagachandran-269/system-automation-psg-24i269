# Robotic arm joint health monitor

Run `python generate_arm_history.py` to recreate the deterministic synthetic history, then run `python robotic_arm_health.py --demo` from this directory. The history contains 360 labeled joint readings (healthy or worn) across shoulder, elbow, and wrist joints. The monitor trains a standardized 9-nearest-neighbors classifier on torque, temperature, and vibration and combines its per-joint wear probabilities into one arm-level decision. The implementation uses only the Python standard library. `maintenance_log.txt` is a sample plain-sentence log; each note names an arm so it can be associated with that arm without turning the note itself into structured sensor data.

The immediate-stop rule is deterministic and runs before any model call: torque at or above 180 Nm, temperature at or above 100 C, or vibration at or above 12 mm/s on any joint produces `EMERGENCY_STOP`. These deliberately conservative, illustrative trip points sit above the synthetic normal and worn ranges, so predictive wear can be flagged before reaching them. They are engineering examples only; deployed limits must be set and validated against the robot, load, sensor calibration, and the manufacturer's safety envelope. A monitor created with `history_file=None` demonstrates that the emergency trip still operates without a trained model. If neither a model nor a meaningful technician note is available, the system raises an error rather than misreporting `SAFE`.

The model schedules maintenance at a maximum joint wear probability of 0.70, and requests monitoring from 0.40 up to that point. Technician notes are a second, independent rule-based signal: explicit mechanical symptoms such as grinding or a crack schedule maintenance, while weaker symptoms such as a rattle request monitoring. Either can escalate a low-risk sensor/model result; notes cannot downgrade a sensor alert. Immediate sensor trips and suspected spoofing take precedence over all other decisions.

The spoof defense compares each current joint torque with its previous reading. A drop of more than 55% from a prior torque of at least 35 Nm, while that joint still has temperature of at least 70 C or vibration of at least 6 mm/s, triggers an emergency stop. This targets an abrupt low-torque spoof intended to hide an ongoing stressed joint. It does not prove malicious intent and cannot reliably catch gradual drift, a spoof present before a trustworthy baseline exists, or a coordinated attack that also falsifies temperature and vibration. Independent authenticated sensing and safety-rated hardware interlocks are still required.

The demo prints `SAFE`, `MONITOR`, `SCHEDULE_MAINTENANCE`, and `EMERGENCY_STOP`, plus a separate spoof-detection example. The synthetic data and simple text rules demonstrate a pipeline, not a validated industrial safety system.
